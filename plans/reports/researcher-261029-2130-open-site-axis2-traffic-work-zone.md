# Axis 2 — Public Traffic Interface (TTM / Work Zone) for enterprise linear projects

Date 2026-10-29. Researcher. Scope: boundary between open linear site and live traffic.
Evidence tags in Source column: **[F]** page/PDF opened and read this session; **[S]** seen only in a search-result snippet (NOT opened; treat as UNVERIFIED, see §5); **[L]** local PDF read via pdftotext.
Prediction before research: expect hard crash-reduction numbers for intrusion alarms/AI. Falsified: none found. Evidence for intrusion tech is thin (cost, false alarms, small samples); strongest numbers are for queue warning + automated speed enforcement.

## 1. Executive summary

- Gap confirmed and large. Closed-site stack (TP1.2/3.3/3.5) has zero touchpoint with traffic. Vietnam evidence it matters: Noi Bai–Lao Cai widening logged 31 collisions/crashes, 6 injured, 1 dead in ~3 months, plus 30+ collisions in Q1 2026 [F: baolaocai.vn x2].
- Scale elsewhere: US 891 work-zone deaths in 2022, 742 (83%, calculated) drivers/passengers, 21% of fatal crashes rear-end [F: https://ops.fhwa.dot.gov/Wz/resources/facts_stats.htm]. UK ~6,500 vehicle incursions into roadworks Oct 2017–Oct 2020 (~175/month) [F]. NEXCO East vehicle-into-closure collisions 35 (2021) -> 53 (2022) [F]. The motorist is the main victim, not only the worker -> enterprise liability is two-sided.
- Best-measured interventions: portable end-of-queue warning (Texas est. -44% crashes [F]; Illinois -14% queuing crashes, preliminary [F]) and work-zone automated speed enforcement (Maryland: ~80% fewer speed violations [F]). Both are CORRIDOR, both are cheap relative to robotics.
- Intrusion alarms (impact cones, hoses, beams): decades of DOT trials, mostly shelved for false alarms, set-up burden, short warning time [L: Oregon DOT 2017]. No credible crash-reduction number found. Newest direction = portable AI camera on the work vehicle: NEXCO Central Japan commercial product (May 2026, ~250 m upstream detection) [F]. Georgia DOT still researching (to 2027) [F].
- Robotics (auto cone-laying, SwiftGate, driverless TMA) exist and are deployed (UK, Colorado) but are justified by US/UK labour-injury economics. Turnkey ATMA retrofit ~US$450k [F]. Low priority for enterprise vs. barrier/sign/lighting compliance (inference: VN labour cost + failure modes seen in VN are basic).
- TP1.2 does not transfer. Fixed cameras + "alert command center / Zalo" fails the physics: at 80–100 km/h a vehicle covers 250 m in ~9–11 s (arithmetic); Oregon DOT field tests measured worker reaction 2–6 s and set 6 s as the target [L], so the alert must be local (siren/strobe/wearable haptic) and reach the worker, not a dashboard. Camera must move with the work front (vehicle/trailer-mounted, battery/solar, edge AI).
- TP3.3 "autonomous heavy machinery" has no world analogue on live roads; the real autonomy in work zones is the leader–follower TMA. Machinery inside the closure needs worker-proximity warning (RFID/UWB), evidence on effect size not found.
- TP3.5 Site OS needs a new object: the TTM plan as live, geolocated record (permit scope, closure chainage, taper position, speed limit, who changed it) + incursion/near-miss ledger. UK keeps incursion database (AIRSweb [S]); FHWA WZDx is the open data format for publishing closures to nav apps [F].
- Vietnam regulation is already explicit: permit + traffic-assurance plan before work (TT 41/2024 Art. 5–6 [L]); a Cục Đường bộ VN document reported by MoC site on 27/05/2026 (number not stated; effective 01/07/2026; addressed to road-management zones and PMUs) requires QCVN 41:2024/BGTVT + TCCS 14:2016/TCĐBVN, arrow-board barriers + flashing amber on expressways, night lighting, penalties under NĐ 336/2025 [F]. Same directive admits temporary signs/barriers/guidance devices "chưa bảo đảm theo quy định" on site. This is the cheapest, most defensible first deliverable.
- Recommendation (ranked): (1) digital TTM compliance + audit layer; (2) portable AI intrusion alert + impact cones for night/narrow closures; (3) end-of-queue warning + temp speed mgmt at transitions; (4) conventional TMA/mobile barrier + proximity warning in closure; (5) WZDx-style feed pilot; (6) defer ATMA, auto-cone, V2X.

## 3. Case studies

1. **Noi Bai–Lao Cai expressway widening 2->4 lanes, Km123+080–244+155 (Vietnam, 2026)** [F]. 31 collisions/crashes, 6 injured, 1 dead in 3+ months; 10 in 14/2–7/3/2026; fines on two contractors. Causes named: narrowed emergency lane, reduced sight distance from dense barriers, frequently changing signs, debris on lanes, speeding, post-Tet traffic. Response: 50 km/h, reflective markers, lighting, cable locks, welded barrier beams, patrols; cameras not mentioned. Lesson: failure is TTM design + behaviour, not worker intrusion alone. https://baolaocai.vn/dam-bao-an-toan-giao-thong-tren-cao-toc-noi-bai-lao-cai-nhung-van-de-dat-ra-post895729.html ; https://baolaocai.vn/tang-cuong-cac-phuong-an-an-toan-giao-thong-tren-tuyen-cao-toc-noi-bai-lao-cai-post898857.html . Sister case: HCMC–Long Thanh–Dau Giay widening to 8–10 lanes, 60 km/h zones, packages XL01/XL02 (Deo Ca / Truong Son JVs), steel-sheet barriers, no crash data published https://thuviennhadat.vn/bat-dong-san/cao-toc-tphcm-long-thanh-dau-giay-han-che-toc-do-60km-h-tai-mot-so-khu-vuc-thi-cong-rao-chan-va-mo-r-25040.html .
2. **National Highways UK: cone laying -> SwiftGate (2020–2022)** [F]. Government-funded innovation fund, contractor-built (Highway Care, Kier, King, Balfour Beatty). Spec-driven procurement (400 cones, 10 s/cone, safe for all road users). Pattern worth copying: owner sets performance spec, contractors compete; staged off-road -> on-road trials. https://www.gov.uk/government/news/automated-cone-laying-vehicle-spotted-on-roads-in-new-trial ; https://gov.uk/government/news/super-swift-gates-which-automatically-close-off-lanes-to-be-trialled-on-national-highways-network
3. **NEXCO Japan: closure-intrusion crisis -> AI product (2021–2026)** [F]. NEXCO East collisions into closures 35 (2021), 53 (2022), 37 in H1 2023; countermeasures 2023: synchronised LED arrow lights, horn speaker from van, stronger taper standard; 2022 demos: laser-on-cone alarm, worker-worn camera AI; May 2026 commercial Highway Ai-MONITOR. Lesson: Japan moved from alarms on cones to AI cameras in ~4 years, portable not fixed. https://trafficnews.jp/post/128712 ; https://trafficnews.jp/post/122597/2 ; https://www.c-nexco.co.jp/corporate/pressroom/news_release/6603.html
4. **Maryland SafeZones (US, since 2010)** [F]. Automated speed enforcement in work zones; ~80% fewer speed violations by 2013 (FHWA); staffed vehicle-mounted units behind barrier; practice limited to >=45 mph roads. Caveat: statewide WZ crashes still rose 9.6% 2014–2017 while WZ events rose 27.1% [S]. https://ops.fhwa.dot.gov/publications/fhwahop17044/index.htm
5. **Colorado DOT ATMA (US, 2017–)** [F]. Leader–follower, striping only, 13-worker survey: positive but "not comfortable in complex situations"; cost and limited use cases are CDOT's stated barriers. Lesson: even the flagship deployment is narrow. https://www.nationalacademies.org/read/27903/chapter/8 ; https://aii.transportation.org/Pages/Autonomous-Truck-Mounted-Attenuator.aspx
6. **Texas portable end-of-queue warning (US)** [F]. Radar + PCMS + rumble strips at nighttime closures; est. -44% crashes; payback 95–190 nights; small sample. https://workzonesafety.org/publication/safety-effects-of-portable-end-of-queue-warning-system-deployments-at-texas-work-zones

## 4. Limits / failure modes

- **Cost.** ATMA ~US$450k retrofit + training ~US$100k; IntelliStrobe ~US$25k/unit [L]. Queue warning cheaper (payback 95–190 nights, Texas) but still per-site kit.
- **False alarms / reliability.** Oregon DOT literature review: intrusion alarms were repeatedly dropped for persistent false alarms from work vehicles, long set-up, short warning time [L]. TDOT/Caltrans reports on Intellicone/WAS false positives and negatives [S]. AI camera: range falls at night, in tunnels and bad weather (vendor's own caveat) [F]; NC test lowest at night in rain [S]. Warning-fatigue risk when marshals/trucks of enterprise itself keep triggering.
- **Evidence quality.** Texas -44% and Oregon findings: small samples [F]. Illinois -14%: preliminary, queue comparability unclear [F]. Maryland ASE speed drop is a violation-rate metric, not crashes.
- **Legal.** ASE may need enabling legislation; driverless TMA conflicts with driverless-vehicle rules in some US states [F]. VN: legal basis for contractor-operated speed cameras, ANPR/plate capture, and AI video of public road users not checked (see §6). Personal-data aspects of filming public traffic: unchecked.
- **Behavioural.** Speed-limit signs alone did not stop crashes on Noi Bai–Lao Cai [F]; narrowed lanes and dense barriers themselves reduce sight distance.
- **Adoption trap.** Automation is sold on labour savings; VN labour cost structure differs (inference), so ROI for cone/ATMA robots likely negative vs. discipline + compliance.
- **Gap/cut-in risk.** Passenger vehicles slipping between lead and follower in ATMA [F].
