# Axis 3 — Linear data model + progress + QA (open-site vs closed-site)

Date 2026-10-29. Scope: highways/expressways/bridges. Ledger: FACT = I opened the URL (or extracted the PDF) and read the number; INFERENCE = my reasoning; UNVERIFIED = snippet/vendor-only or not opened. Framing: NODE = closed (casting/precast yard, batching plant, pier pad); CORRIDOR = open (km of earthworks/pavement).

## 1. Executive summary

- Spatial key flips from "floor/zone/BIM element" to **alignment + chainage (Km+m) + offset + layer**. IFC 4.3 (IfcAlignment + IfcLinearPlacement + IfcReferent) is the standard container; ISO-published 2024 (FACT). But **no owner mandate found** for National Highways / Trafikverket / Singapore (UNVERIFIED), and **Japan MLIT explicitly does NOT require IFC for road/earthwork models, uses J-LandXML** because IFC 4.3RC1 lacks Japanese road-design elements (FACT, FY2024 doc). Recommendation: LandXML/alignment+chainage now, IFC 4.3 export as target, not as gate.
- TP1.3 (weekly drone point cloud vs BIM 4D, % per floor) must become **% per chainage segment per layer** (earthwork, subbase, base, AC) vs design surface. Drone is fine at NODE and for earthwork volume; for pavement-layer QA, vehicle mobile LiDAR beat aerial photogrammetry at half the cost per mile in PennDOT test ($3.8k vs $7.8k/mi; vertical RMSE 0.031 vs 0.052 ft) (FACT, 2015, 6 mi).
- **Vietnam drone legal block**: Decree 288/2025 requires a per-mission flight permit from Ministry of National Defence (apply >=7 days ahead, 12 if NOTAM; 5 working days to decide); beyond-visual-range with >=2 kg aircraft needs Class B licence (FACT, partial read). Weekly corridor flights = permit workflow, not a camera problem.
- Biggest measurable QA wins on corridor are **in-process, machine-sourced**: intelligent compaction (roller GNSS + stiffness map; +3-5% roller price, retrofit $50-75k, payback "2 or 3 projects" per FHWA) and paver thermal profiling (US 82: mat temperature range cut >50%). These replace TP1.3-style post-hoc scanning for pavement.
- Closed-site robots (FieldPrinter TP2.2, Spot TP3.1, iPad-AR MEP TP2.4) mostly **KEEP only at NODES** (casting yard, deck, batching plant). On the corridor, layout = GNSS stakeless/machine control; patrol = vehicle-mounted sensors.
- Japan i-Construction: ICT earthwork gave ~30% time saving (MLIT white paper FY2018); levee case 213 -> 142 man-days (-33%), survey 15 -> 6 man-days; target now i-Construction 2.0: -30% labour by FY2040 ("1.5x productivity"). Adoption is machine-guidance + 3D data + owner acceptance rules together, not tools alone.
- Vietnam already has national CORS: VNGEONET, 65 stations (24 geodetic + 41 NRTK), done 2019 (FACT). Corridor machine control can rely on VRS instead of per-site base stations (INFERENCE; coverage along each enterprise route and access terms UNVERIFIED).
- Bridges split into NODE-heavy QA: segment geometry control at the casting yard (sub-mm casting error grows to metres at span end, ISARC paper), concrete maturity sensors (Idaho interchange: 5-10 days saved, 10-15% faster, vendor-reported). These are the strongest "keep digital twin" case; they are NODE.
- Linear scheduling (time-chainage / line-of-balance) is the right progress view, but **I found no independent measured benefit number**; treat as method fit, not ROI.
- Weakest evidence: bridge construction-stage digital twins (only scale-model/academic papers found), mass-haul telematics savings (single vendor story), FAA Part 108 relevance (irrelevant to Vietnam).

## 3. Case studies

1. **Tone River levee, Japan (CORRIDOR, earthwork).** Kanto Regional Bureau; ICT excavation/embankment/slope; stakeless; survey 15 -> 6 man-days, construction 213 -> 142 man-days; no delay near JR main line. i-Construction award document. https://www.mlit.go.jp/report/press/content/001388518.pdf
2. **Georgiou Group, Alkimos, Western Australia (CORRIDOR-like cut/fill).** 600,000 m3, six-month job, mass-haul analysis + payload/cycle tracking; 33% time reduction, 25% haul productivity. Vendor (Trimble/SITECH) source, self-reported. https://sitechcs.com/efficiency-helps-complete-earthworks-project-early/
3. **PennDOT I-81/I-84, USA (CORRIDOR).** 24-mile LAMP project with 6-mile mobile LiDAR comparison: $3,800 vs $7,800/mi, RMSE 0.031 vs 0.052 ft. https://informedinfrastructure.com/index.php/post/mobile-lidar-proves-cost-effective-for-pavement-mapping
4. **TxDOT US 82 PAVE-IR pilot and Washington US-12 failure, USA (CORRIDOR paving).** Mat temperature range reduced >50% after process changes; US-12 failure 5 years early, ~$2.4M. https://www.asphaltmagazine.com/infrared-system-detects-thermal-segregation/
5. **C. L. Heilman, Idaho interchange bridge, USA (NODE/deck+piers).** Maturity sensing; 5-10 days saved, 10-15% faster (vendor-reported). https://www.casestudies.com/company/giatec/case-study/c-l-heilman-company-inc-customer-case-study
6. **VNGEONET, Vietnam (enabler for CORRIDOR machine control).** 65-station national GNSS network (24 geodetic + 41 NRTK), completed 2019, VN2000-tied. https://leica-geosystems.com/case-studies/surveying-and-engineering/advancing-vietnam-geodetic

## 4. Limits / failure modes

- **Evidence is mostly US/Japan agency or vendor-authored.** Only three measured effects are from public-sector sources (Japan MLIT, FHWA cost premium, PennDOT). Georgiou, Giatec, MOBA are vendor/self-reported.
- **IC cost claim.** Tennessee "nearly 50%" cost reduction is one agency abstract; FHWA's own overview (2010) lists benefits without quantification. Search summaries cited a Wyoming "54% / $15,385 per lane-mile" figure that I could not trace (the 55048 record is Tennessee). Do not use it.
- **IC value vs density.** IC stiffness is not density; correlation varies with layer, moisture, roller settings. Plan local calibration; keep cores/nuclear gauge for acceptance until the owner accepts IC data.
- **IFC lock-in risk.** Japan shows an advanced owner not accepting IFC for roads. Vietnam owner acceptance unknown.
- **Drone friction.** Weekly corridor flights depend on MND permits (7-12 day lead) and licence class; one refused permit blanks a week of KPI. Dust/rain/tropical weather also not studied here.
- **CORS dependence.** VNGEONET covers the country nominally, but mountain/cell-dead segments of enterprise routes need a local base; VRS needs data link. Not checked.
- **Mobile LiDAR needs control and traffic management**; PennDOT noted unresolved control-layout question.
- **Bridge digital twins.** Found only scale-model/academic papers; no field-measured construction-stage benefit, so do not sell them as ROI.
- **"Progress %" semantics.** A km of earthwork "50% done" depends on layer, haul distance, weather; avoid a single % per km. Prefer per-layer, per-chainage-cell status.
